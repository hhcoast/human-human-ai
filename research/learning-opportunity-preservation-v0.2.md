# Human–Human–AI v0.2 | Learning Opportunity Preservation
**Status:** exploratory design proposal, 14 November 2026. Not a validated method or effectiveness finding.

## Research question
Under what conditions does AI-assisted task automation preserve, weaken or strengthen opportunities to develop independent human judgment, interpersonal learning and consequential capability?

## Definitions and distinctions
- **Task performance:** quality, speed or completeness of the immediate AI-assisted result.
- **Learning:** observable change in understanding or competence measured on subsequent tasks.
- **Judgment:** ability to assess evidence, recognise uncertainty, consider alternatives and justify choices.
- **Agency:** effective opportunity to challenge, correct, decline, intervene and shape the resulting action.
- **Learning Opportunity Preservation (LOP):** a proposed design check to identify learning functions of tasks altered by AI, and preserve, scaffold, reconstruct or augment these learning functions. This is not a validated construct.
- **Cognitive offloading** must not be conflated with learning loss or long-term capability erosion.

## Proposed transition chain
Human context and intention → Human–Human–AI interaction → Learning and capability formation → Independent judgment → Authority and intervention → Observable consequence → feedback and revision.

These arrows are hypotheses requiring observation, not proven causal effects. Institutional access, fairness, accessibility and the right to challenge apply across the chain.

## LOP reflection prompts
1. What actual work is delegated to AI?
2. Which opportunities to practise, receive feedback, make mistakes or encounter alternatives may change?
3. Which practices should be preserved, scaffolded, reconstructed or augmented?
4. What is the role of another human, such as a mentor or peer?
5. Can the learner apply and explain the capability on a different task without equivalent assistance?
6. Can a human disagreement or correction affect the workflow outcome?

## Evaluation outline
- Baseline task without AI: competence, explanations, uncertainty.
- Compare independent attempt, direct AI solutions and structured collaborative AI support, where feasible and ethical.
- Separate immediate task quality from delayed unaided transfer (e.g., after one week).
- Assess reasoning against pre-specified subject-matter rubrics; record error detection, justified deviation, feedback and intervention outcomes.
- For small pilots (4–6 participants), use descriptive and qualitative conclusions only; avoid causal or population-level effectiveness claims.
- Participation must be voluntary. Collect only the minimum necessary data, with explicit permission and a clear deletion policy.

## Evidence register, initial primary sources
1. **Anthropic (2026), AI assistance and coding skills:** experimental study of AI support and short-term skill formation in a coding context. Limit: small domain-specific study and immediate outcome. https://www.anthropic.com/research/AI-assistance-coding-skills
2. **Microsoft Research / CHI (2025), critical thinking and generative AI:** survey of knowledge workers and self-reported cognitive effort. Limit: correlational self-reports, not demonstration of durable loss. https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/the-impact-of-generative-ai-on-critical-thinking-self-reported-reductions-in-cognitive-effort-and-confidence-effects-from-a-survey-of-knowledge-workers/
3. **OECD Digital Education Outlook (2026):** evidence synthesis distinguishing improved outputs from learning gains; quality depends on instructional design and context. https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/2026/01/oecd-digital-education-outlook-2026_940e0dd8.html
4. **PwC US (9 November 2026), Critical Thinking & Judgment Certificate:** organisation-level program announcement, **not** evidence of impact. https://www.pwc.com/us/en/about-us/newsroom/critical-thinking-and-judgment-certificate-program.html
5. **UNESCO AI Competency Framework for Teachers:** normative competence orientation, not an effect evaluation. https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/ai-competency-framework-teachers
6. **W3C WCAG 2.2:** normative accessibility reference; conformance requires separate testing. https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/

## Website scope of this branch
- Standalone German and English choice-based scenario: `learning-opportunities.html`, `en/learning-opportunities.html`.
- Informed selection, explanatory feedback, optional open reflection and local-only clipboard copy.

- No external libraries, tracking, persistence or remote user-data submission.

## Decisions to avoid
Do not present reflections as scores, diagnoses, certificates or evidence of learning. Do not claim that removing an old repetitive task necessarily causes skill loss. Do not infer actual effectiveness from clicks, completion or satisfaction.
