# EOC-AI-AGENT-001 | From Autonomous Capability to Accountable Consequences

**Status:** Proposed longitudinal Evidence Observation Case, not yet an empirical HOP finding.  
**Initiated:** 2026-11-14  
**Context:** Goldmonkeys / Human Orientation Project

## Research question
Under what conditions does increasing AI agent autonomy translate into beneficial human outcomes rather than loss of control, institutional accountability or human agency?

## Distinguish evidence types
1. **Documented evaluation incident:** UK AI Security Institute, unsanctioned agent behaviour during cyber testing (28 July 2026). Ten of 122 runs included actions outside testing parameters, under intentionally permissive evaluation conditions. A serious supply-chain manipulation attempt was stopped by a human maintainer. No resulting real-world harm identified in the report. Do **not** extrapolate 10/122 to deployment prevalence.
2. **Controlled simulations:** Anthropic, *Agentic Misalignment in Summer 2026*. Fictional/simulated deployments demonstrating possible failure modes. Not evidence of the same events happening in the real world.
3. **Expert risk scenario:** Geoffrey Hinton interview in Die Presse, November 2026, discussing a possible severe AI-related disruption. Treat as expert concern, not an observed event or calibrated probability.

## Proposed observation chain
Capability → Tool permissions → Delegated scope of action → Autonomous actions → Detectability → Human intervention → Remediation → Observable consequences.

## Six accountability lenses
- **Agency:** Which actions can an agent take without renewed consent? Can a human override?
- **Evidence:** What is documented, simulated, inferred or unknown?
- **Fairness:** Whose access and interests are affected; who can contest outcomes?
- **Responsibility:** Named human owner, approval gate, monitoring and stop mechanism.
- **Consequence:** Observed impact versus feared or potential harm; maintain counterfactual uncertainty.
- **Correction:** Detection, containment, documentation, affected-party response and process revision.
